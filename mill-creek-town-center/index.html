Plumber in Mill Creek Town Center, Mill Creek, WA

Mill Creek Town Center is the walkable shopping and dining district in the middle of Mill Creek, WA. The City of Mill Creek describes it as a lifestyle center with more than 80 shops, restaurants and services, and condos and townhomes sit close around it. If a plumbing problem hits a unit, a shop or a kitchen here, call and tell us the building and what you see.

This page explains what makes plumbing different in a mixed commercial and residential district, who is usually responsible for which pipe, and what the city has said about where the district is heading.

Where Town Center Sits In Mill Creek

Town Center is built along Main Street, close to Bothell-Everett Highway (SR 527). Library Park, at 15523 Bothell-Everett Highway, is one of the named public spaces nearby. History sources say construction of the center began in 2001, with later phases finishing in 2007, so most of the buildings are newer than the original homes in the city.

  • What it isA lifestyle center with more than 80 shops, restaurants and services, according to the City of Mill Creek.
  • Housing nearbyLow-rise condos near the center and townhomes across the city, according to a Homes.com neighborhood guide.
  • Main roadsMain Street and Bothell-Everett Highway (SR 527).
  • ZIP codeStreet addresses in Mill Creek use ZIP 98012.

Because the district mixes businesses and homes, the plumbing questions here are less about the age of a house and more about shared systems, tenant rules and heavy daily use.

What The City Is Planning South Of Town Center

The city runs a South Town Center Subarea project. The area stretches west from Bothell-Everett Highway to North Creek, and south from City Hall North to the QFC building on the south side of 164th Street. The city says it wants a walkable district with new housing, shops, jobs and public spaces, built in phases.

That is a planning goal, not a construction schedule. The city says the work would be coordinated with road and stormwater systems. If you own property in that area, check the city's project page before you plan major changes to a building.

Mixed-Use Buildings: Who Owns Which Pipe

In a building with shops below and homes above, one drain stack can serve several tenants. A backup in your unit may start in a shared line that belongs to the building, not to you. Leases and condo documents usually say where the owner's duty ends and the tenant's begins.

Read your lease or HOA rules before you pay for work, and tell the plumber what they say. It also helps to find your unit's shutoff in advance. Many multi-unit buildings have one for each unit and one for the whole building, and the manager usually knows where they are.

Restaurant And Shop Plumbing

Kitchens send grease, food solids and heat into the drains every hour. A slow floor drain or a gurgling sink in a food business is often a grease buildup in the line. Hydro jetting scours the inside of a greasy pipe, and a camera shows whether grease is the only problem.

Food service spaces typically need a grease trap or interceptor under the plumbing code, and the sewer provider sets rules for keeping it serviced. Ask the building department and your sewer provider what applies to your space. Our commercial plumbing page lists the other jobs we take calls about, such as water heaters, restroom fixtures and leaks.

Condos And Townhomes Near The Center

Condos and townhomes share walls, floors and sometimes supply lines. A leak in one unit can reach another, so speed matters and so does telling the neighbor. Leak detection helps when the wet spot shows up far from the source, such as a stain on a ceiling below.

In many condos the water heater sits in a small closet. A water heater repair or a replacement may need approval from the HOA, so ask the manager before you book the work.

Water And Sewer Providers

The City of Mill Creek's utilities page lists two providers for the city, Alderwood Water and Wastewater District and Silver Lake Water and Sewer District. Which one serves a building depends on the address, so check a recent bill. Washington's plumbing code, chapter 51-56 of the Washington Administrative Code, is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code, and the city's building department can say whether your job needs a permit.

Before You Call About A Leak Or Backup

  1. Find the shutoff. Look for the valve under the sink or at the fixture first. If water is still flowing, ask the building manager for the unit or building shutoff.
  2. Stop using the drain. A backup that is still draining into shared pipes can spill into another unit. Wait until a plumber has looked.
  3. Note where it shows. Write down which fixtures are slow or wet and whether neighbors have the same problem. That points to a shared line.
  4. Check who is responsible. Look at your lease or HOA rules, then tell the plumber what they say before work starts.

Mill Creek Town Center Plumbing Questions

Do you work on plumbing in Mill Creek Town Center?

Call and give us the building address and the problem. We will tell you whether we can help with it and what to expect before you decide.

Who pays when a drain backs up in a shared building?

It depends on where the blockage is. A clog in a line that serves only your unit is usually yours to fix, and a clog in a shared stack is usually the owner's. Your lease or condo documents decide, so check them and tell the plumber.

Is Town Center a residential neighborhood?

Mostly it is a commercial and mixed-use district. The city calls it a lifestyle center with shops and restaurants, with condos and townhomes close by. For single-family homes around the golf course, see our Mill Creek Country Club area page.

Does a Mill Creek address always mean I am inside the City of Mill Creek?

No. ZIP 98012 also covers Mill Creek East, a census-designated place southeast of the city that is not part of the City of Mill Creek. Ask the city and Snohomish County which permit office covers your address.

What is the South Town Center Subarea plan?

It is a city planning project for the land between Bothell-Everett Highway and North Creek, south of City Hall North. The city says it aims for a walkable district with new housing, shops and public spaces, built in phases. The city's project page has the current details.
