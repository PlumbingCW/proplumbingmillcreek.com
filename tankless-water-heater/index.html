Tankless Water Heater in Mill Creek, WA

A tankless water heater in Mill Creek, WA heats water on demand through a heat exchanger instead of storing it in a tank, and it needs its own kind of care. We service, repair, flush and install tankless units, and the 24/7 line takes calls about them.

Many owners come to us after another company declined the job but offered to replace the unit. We will look at yours first, tell you what we find and say honestly when we cannot help.

Tankless flush and descale

A flush circulates a descaling solution through the heat exchanger to dissolve mineral buildup. We connect a pump and hoses to the service valves on the unit, run the solution for a set time, rinse, and check the inlet filter. It is a routine job, and it is often all an owner needs.

Scale builds up faster in some water than others. Signs include a drop in hot water temperature, a unit that cycles on and off, error codes that point to flow or temperature, and louder operation. Ask your water utility or check your latest water quality report if you want to know what your supply carries.

Not every unit has service valves. Without them, a flush is harder, and installing valves is an extra step we explain before we start. We do not open the heater's cabinet for a flush, and you should not either.

Error codes, beeping and a unit that is not working

Tankless units tell you what is wrong in codes on a display or in blinking lights. The manual lists them, and most codes point to a small number of causes: ignition, flame sensing, flow, exhaust, a temperature sensor or a condensate drain.

Check the easy things. Is the gas supply open? Is the cold water on? Is the unit plugged in or the breaker on? Is the vent clear outside? A blocked condensate line or a closed valve causes many codes.

Stop there. Do not open the cabinet, and do not handle the unit's wiring or circuit boards. If you have already opened it and found burnt wiring or a scorched smell, switch the power off and call us. That is a repair for a technician, and it may mean the board or the whole unit.

Tell the dispatcher the make, model and the exact code. That lets us bring the right parts and check whether your brand is one we can service.

Tankless service, repair and installation

We handle the usual repairs: igniters, flame rods, sensors, fans, flow sensors, condensate traps and inlet filters. We also install new units, and swap a tank for a tankless when the house suits it.

Some repairs are worth doing and some are not. Parts for older units can be hard to find, and a major failure on a unit in its second decade may cost more than a replacement. We tell you when that is the case, rather than bill you for a repair that will not hold.

For a leak at a tankless unit, shut off the water and power to it and call us. A leak from a union or valve is common and fixable, but a leak from the heat exchanger itself is usually a replacement.

Why some companies decline tankless service

Tankless heaters need brand-specific knowledge, test equipment and parts, and many plumbers just do not stock or train for them. Some prefer to replace rather than repair. That is a business choice, and it leaves owners with a unit that might have needed only a flush.

We look at the unit first. If a flush, a filter clean or a small part solves it, that is what we recommend. If we cannot service your brand, we tell you so on the phone, so you are not paying for a visit that ends with no answer.

Is a tankless heater right for my house?

Tankless suits some homes and not others. Endless hot water sounds appealing, but the unit needs a gas line big enough to feed it, a vent that fits, a place on the wall and an electrical outlet for the controls. Electric tankless units need very large circuits that many panels cannot spare.

Gas line and venting sizing

A tankless burner draws far more gas at once than a tank burner. The line from the meter, its length and any appliances sharing it all count. If your line is undersized, the unit may not fire at full output, and you get lukewarm water at high demand.

We look at the existing line and talk through whether it needs changing. Gas work may involve other licensed steps, and we say so up front. Permit rules vary, so ask the building department for your address.

Venting is the other limit. Tankless vents are different from tank vents, so an old chimney connection usually has to change. Plan for a side-wall vent and a condensate drain.

Is my old tankless worth keeping?

Some tankless units run well for a long time with regular flushing. A unit around 18 years old that still heats well and has been serviced is often worth another flush and a look. One that throws codes constantly, leaks from the exchanger or has a known parts shortage may not be.

We tell you which side yours falls on. Ask us to look before you decide, because a flush costs less than a new unit.

Mill Creek homes and tankless

Original-plat homes from the late 1970s and 1980s often have a tank in a closet with a chimney vent. Moving to tankless means a new vent path and a gas line check. Newer parts of the city, including Town Center area homes and The Farm at Mill Creek, are likelier to have a layout planned for a wall unit.

Western Washington is wet in fall through spring. A condensate drain outside can drip onto a path, and a vent terminal near a planter can be blocked by growth. We look at both when we plan a unit.

Tankless water heater questions

How much does a tankless flush cost?

The price depends on whether the unit has service valves, how much scale there is, the make and the access. We give a clear price before work starts, and we do not quote a flat number sight unseen.

Do you only replace tankless units, or do you service them?

We service them. We flush, descale, diagnose codes and repair. If a unit is worn out we say so, but we start with what can be saved.

Can I open my tankless heater to check it?

No. Leave the cabinet closed and do not touch the wiring or boards. Check the gas, water, power and vent outside, then call us.

Can you help same day?

Sometimes, when available. The 24/7 line takes tankless calls, and we tell you what is possible once we know the make, the code and the address.

Is a tankless heater right for an older Mill Creek home?

It can be, but the gas line and the vent are the big questions. We look at both before recommending anything, and we say when a tank is the better fit.

Can you repair any brand?

Not always. Some brands need special tools or parts. Tell the dispatcher the make and model, and we will say whether we can service it.

My unit is about 18 years old. Is it worth flushing?

Often yes if it still heats and has no leak. A flush is a modest job compared with a replacement. We will tell you after we look.

Can you service a tankless unit at my rental or shop?

Yes. Tell us who approves the work, and give us the model and the address.
