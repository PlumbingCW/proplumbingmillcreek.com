Contact Mill Creek Pro Plumbing

The fastest way to reach a plumber in Mill Creek, WA is to call the 24/7 line. A person picks up at any hour, day or night, weekends and holidays included.

This site has no contact form on purpose. A phone call gets a leak or a backup in front of a dispatcher in seconds.

How to reach us

Press any Call button on this page and you will reach the 24/7 line. Call the 24/7 line for emergencies such as a burst pipe, a sewer backup or a water heater leaking onto the floor. Call it for planned work too, such as a new faucet or a water heater swap.

We don't take service requests by email or by form. Our email address, contact@proplumbingmillcreek.com, is listed here as plain text for general questions only. If water is running or a drain is backing up, don't wait for an email reply. Call.

What to have ready when you call

A few details let the dispatcher understand the job in the first minute. You don't need to know the cause or the right plumbing words. Describe what you see, hear or smell.

  • The address where the plumber should go, including the unit or apartment number.
  • What is happening: a leak, a clog, no hot water, a smell, a noise in the walls.
  • Whether the water is still running, and whether you found or closed the shutoff.
  • Which fixtures are affected. One slow sink and every drain in the house are very different problems.
  • A phone number where you can be reached on site. Photos are not needed.

If you are not sure about any of these, call anyway. The dispatcher will ask questions one at a time and work out the rest with you.

What to do while you wait

Stop the water if you can.

Find the main shutoff, often near where the supply line enters the house or in a garage or crawlspace, and turn it off. If it won't close, leave it and tell us.

Keep people away from wet electrical areas.

Don't touch outlets, power strips or panels near standing water. If water is reaching them, turn off power only if you can do so while standing on dry ground.

Stop using the affected drains.

Running a washer or flushing into a backed-up line only adds to the mess. Move valuables and rugs out of the splash zone.

Gas smell? Leave first.

Get everyone out of the building and call 911 or the gas utility from outside, before you call us.

Before you choose any plumber

Washington's Department of Labor and Industries offers a public tool called Verify a Contractor, Tradesperson, or Business. It shows whether a contractor is registered and whether a registration is suspended, along with the bond and insurance information on file.

We encourage you to use it with us and with anyone else you are considering. Our about page describes how a call works and what we do not promise. If you are still deciding which service you need, the plumbing services page lists every job we take.

Contact questions

Can I request a plumber by email?

No. Email is not monitored for service requests, because a plumbing problem can get worse by the minute. Call the 24/7 line instead and a dispatcher will start the process right away.

Can I visit your office to ask about a job?

Not as a walk-in. The address is a business address, and we work at customers' homes and businesses. A phone call is the quickest way to get an answer on your job.

When will I hear the price?

You get a clear price before work starts. The plumber assesses the problem, explains the cost and your options, and begins only after you agree. We can't quote exact figures over the phone for a problem nobody has seen.

Do you answer at night and on holidays?

Yes. The line is staffed 24/7. Same-day appointments are offered when available, but we don't promise an arrival time, since it depends on the job, traffic and how many calls are waiting.
