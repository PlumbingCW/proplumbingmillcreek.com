Plumber Near Mill Creek Country Club, Mill Creek, WA

Mill Creek began as a golf course community, and the private Mill Creek Country Club and golf course are the landmark the city itself uses to describe how the community started. If you live on the tree-lined streets around the course and need a plumber, call and tell us your street.

This page covers what the city and local history sources say about the area, how to think about plumbing in homes built over several decades, and what to check before you hire anyone.

How The Golf Course Community Began

The City of Mill Creek says the community was originally developed as a golf course community. History sources describe a planned community conceived in the 1970s, with golf course and country club construction starting in early 1975 and the first homes under construction by 1976. The city incorporated on September 30, 1983.

  • LandmarkThe private Mill Creek Country Club and golf course, labeled on the city's housing division map.
  • Original platWikipedia reports 21 subdivisions named for types of trees east of SR 527.
  • GrowthCensus counts of 7,172 residents in 1990, 11,525 in 2000 and 20,926 in 2020.
  • HousingSingle-family homes, townhomes and single-level condos near the course, according to Homes.com.

Homes here were built across several decades, not in one burst. The city added thousands of residents during the 1990s alone. We do not know the exact age of your home, and neither does this page, so a walkthrough is the only way to learn what is inside your walls.

Subdivisions Around The Course

The city's housing division map lists dozens of named subdivisions. Names on it include Fairway, The Masters, Greens, Evergreen, Huckleberry and Cottonwood. A subdivision name does not tell us your exact street, so give the plumber your address instead of a subdivision name.

One brokerage lists Fairwood Greens Townhomes as built in 1988 with 34 units. Treat listing details like that as a starting point. Only permit records and an actual look at the pipes can confirm what a home contains.

Plumbing In Homes Of Different Ages

Age alone does not tell you what pipes you have. Homes from the 1970s and 1980s may have been repiped or remodeled since, and newer homes can have problems too. A plumber should identify the pipe material by looking at it, not by guessing from the year.

Good places to start are the water line where it enters the house, the pipes under the sinks and the ones in a crawlspace or garage. Repiping is a big decision. A close look at the pipes and the water pressure should come before anyone recommends it.

Trees, Roots And Drains

A Homes.com guide describes forested streets and a tall tree canopy around the course. Tree roots seek moisture and can enter small gaps in an older sewer line. That is general plumbing knowledge, not a claim about any one street in Mill Creek.

Slow drains in several fixtures at once, gurgling sounds and a damp patch in the lawn are the usual signs. A sewer camera inspection shows what is in the line, and drain cleaning clears the blockage. If a clog keeps coming back, ask for the camera look first.

Condos And Townhomes Near The Course

Homes.com describes single-level condos near the private course. In a condo the HOA usually controls the building's main shutoff and sometimes the water heater location. Call the manager before you call the plumber, and ask about rules for contractor access and working hours.

A failing water heater gives warning signs in any type of home. Rusty hot water, drips at the base, rumbling noises and a tank older than about ten years are all worth a look.

Water And Sewer Providers And Permits

The City of Mill Creek lists two utility providers, Alderwood Water and Wastewater District and Silver Lake Water and Sewer District. Your water bill shows which one serves you. Washington's plumbing code, chapter 51-56 of the Washington Administrative Code, is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code, and the city's building department can say whether your job needs a permit.

Before You Call About A Plumbing Problem

  1. Find the main shutoff. It is usually where the water line enters the house. Turn it clockwise if water is spreading, and do not force a stuck valve.
  2. Stop using the affected drain. Water that cannot go down can spill out of the lowest fixture in the house.
  3. Take a photo. Pictures of the leak, the stain or the water heater label help the plumber plan before arriving.
  4. Have your address ready. Give the street address and the nearest cross street instead of a subdivision name.

Mill Creek Country Club Area Plumbing Questions

Is the Mill Creek Country Club area an official neighborhood?

The city's map labels the country club and golf course and lists many subdivisions, but we did not find an official neighborhood boundary for this name. We use it as a locator for the streets around the course, so give us your street address.

How old are the homes near the golf course?

They were built over several decades. History sources say the first homes were under construction by 1976, and the city grew quickly through the 1990s. Your own home may be older or newer, so ask a plumber to look.

Do tree roots cause sewer problems in this area?

Roots can enter older sewer lines anywhere trees grow near them. A camera inspection shows whether roots are the cause for your home. We make no claim about any particular street.

Does a Mill Creek mailing address mean I am inside the city?

Not always. ZIP 98012 also covers Mill Creek East, a census-designated place southeast of the city that is not part of the City of Mill Creek. The city and Snohomish County can tell you which one covers your address.

Who is responsible for the water line between the meter and my house?

Often the water provider handles the meter and the main, and the homeowner handles the line from there. Rules differ by provider, so ask yours. Our water service line repair page explains the work involved.
